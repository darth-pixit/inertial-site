The switching moment

Your whole history, home in one upload.

Ask Strava for your archive, upload the ZIP once, and we do the rest.

  1. Ask for your archive. In Strava: Settings → My account → "Download or delete your account". The ZIP lands in your email.

  2. Upload it once. Resumable, so a flaky connection can't kill it. A 2 GB, eight-year archive is done in under 15 minutes.

  3. Everything comes home. Every run, PR, photo and shoe, re-timed by our engine with the differences shown.

FIT, GPX and TCX archives from watches and other apps work the same way. Anything unreadable lands in a "needs attention" list with a reason and a retry.

FAQ

Fair questions.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

Free, forever: training load, fitness & freshness, best efforts, grade-adjusted pace. No ads, no premium tier, no selling your data. Race organizers will pay for registrations; athletes never will. If that ever changes, we'll say so first.

How do I bring in my old runs?

In Strava: Settings → My account → "Download or delete your account", and the ZIP arrives by email. Upload it here once and every run, PR and photo comes home, re-timed by our engine. FIT, GPX and TCX archives from other apps and watches work the same way.

What do you do with my location data?

It stays yours. A privacy zone hides the 200 m around home from every shared map, photos lose their location before they leave your phone, and nothing about where you run feeds suggestions.

Android or iOS?

Both, Android first for polish, with an Apple Watch app on the way. Sign in with Google, Apple or your phone number. No passwords.

When do I get in?

Batch by batch, a small club first. Join the waitlist and we'll message you on WhatsApp when your spot opens.

How we earn your trust

Honesty is the whole product.

Running apps quietly smooth your pace, drop your points and map your front door. Inertial does the opposite, and shows its work.

Accuracy first

If we correct it, we show it.

GPS lies sometimes. When it does, we fix the track and keep the raw trace in grey on every run. Every dropped point carries a reason.

That spike at km 4? It would have meant 47 km/h for a second. We flagged it, fixed it, and kept the original so you can check our work.
Privacy first

Your data. Yours by right.

🏠

Your home stays off the map. A privacy zone hides the 200 m around it from every shared map — on by default, for everyone who isn't you.

📷

Photos lose their location. Location data is stripped from every photo before it leaves your phone.

🔒

Plain-language privacy. Every setting explains itself in one sentence.

👥

You choose who sees each run. 🔒 only you, 👥 followers, or 🌍 everyone — chosen explicitly at sign-up, changeable per run.

Suggestions come from races you both ran, contacts matched as hashes (never uploaded), and mutual follows. Never from where you run.
Live beacon

One WhatsApp link. They watch you run.

Share a beacon before a long run or race. Family opens the link, no account or app, and sees your live dot, pace and distance until you're done. The link is an unlisted random token that expires soon after you finish, and your start point is fuzzed like every shared map.
