Glossary

Grade-adjusted pace

Your pace on a hill converted to the flat-road pace of equal effort. A 5% climb usually costs 30-45 s/km (48-72 s/mi).

By Parth DixitPublished 1 September 2026

What it is

A translation. It takes the pace you actually ran on a slope and reports the flat pace that would have cost the same energy.

The conversion rests on measured energy costs of running at different gradients. Minetti and colleagues published the reference curve in 2002, from treadmill work across slopes from -45% to +45%.

GradientRough pace cost per kmPer mile
+2%12-18 s19-29 s
+5%30-45 s48-72 s
+10%70-95 s113-153 s
-5%10-20 s faster16-32 s faster

What it is not

It is not your race pace on that course. Adjusted pace tells you about effort, not about the clock. A hilly marathon is still slower.

It is also not exact. Downhill running has a limit past which braking costs more than gravity gives, and the curve flattens.

What moves it

  • The gradient itself. The whole input.
  • Elevation data quality. Barometric readings beat satellite ones.
  • Where the slope sits. A climb late in a run costs more than the model says.
  • Your downhill technique. The model assumes you use the descent.

When to ignore it

Ignore it on trails and technical ground. Footing costs energy the gradient model knows nothing about.

Ignore it for pacing a race. Run the climbs on effort, and judge the day on finish time.
