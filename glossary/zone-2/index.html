Glossary

Zone 2

The easy band, usually 60-70% of maximum heart rate. It is meant to be the pace at which you can hold a conversation for the whole run.

By Parth DixitPublished 1 September 2026

What it is

The easy end of the effort scale. In a five-zone model it usually means 60 to 70% of your maximum heart rate.

The point of it is volume. Most of the aerobic benefit of running comes from time spent at an effort you can repeat tomorrow. Seiler's work on elite athletes found roughly 80% of their sessions sat at this kind of easy effort.

What it is not

It is not a magic intensity, and it is not a rule you are failing at. The common complaint is real: _my easy-run heart rate is 160 and I cannot stay in zone 2_. Four things cause that, in this order of likelihood.

  1. Your maximum is set from a formula and is too low for you.
  2. The run is on a hot day, or you are tired, or the route climbs.
  3. Your watch is reading your cadence rather than your pulse.
  4. You are running too fast.

Only the fourth is a fitness problem, and it is the one people assume first.

What moves it

  • The maximum your zones are built on. The biggest lever by far.
  • Heat. Expect 5 to 10 bpm more at 30 °C (86 °F) than at 15 °C (59 °F).
  • Sleep and illness. Both lift heart rate at a fixed pace.
  • Hills. Even a gentle climb pushes you a zone up.

When to ignore it

Ignore it if the talk test says otherwise. Speaking in whole sentences is the better signal, and it needs no calibration.

Ignore it in the first two weeks of running. Early on, almost nothing lands in zone 2, and walking breaks are the fix rather than a failure.
