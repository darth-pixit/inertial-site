Glossary

Dew point

The temperature at which air becomes saturated. Above about 18 °C (64 °F), sweat evaporates poorly and running pace costs rise sharply.

By Parth DixitPublished 1 September 2026

What it is

The temperature at which air can hold no more water. It is a better guide to how a run will feel than relative humidity, because it does not change as the air warms through the day.

Cooling depends on sweat evaporating. When the dew point is high, it does not evaporate well, so you keep the heat.

Dew pointHow it runs
under 10 °C (50 °F)no noticeable cost
13-16 °C (55-61 °F)slightly sticky
16-18 °C (61-64 °F)pace starts to cost
18-21 °C (64-70 °F)hard efforts get much harder
over 21 °C (70 °F)reconsider the session

What it is not

It is not humidity. A relative humidity of 90% at 8 °C (46 °F) is a pleasant morning. The same figure at 28 °C (82 °F) is not.

It is also not the whole story. Ely and colleagues found that slower runners lose more to heat than faster ones over a marathon.

What moves it

  • Air mass, not your route. It is a property of the weather.
  • Time of day, a little. It moves far less than temperature does.
  • Sun and wind. Both change what a given dew point feels like.

When to ignore it

Ignore it for easy running. Slow down and the day is fine.

Ignore the exact number in favour of the sum of temperature and dew point, which is the index most heat adjusters use.

Sources

  • El Helou N et al. Impact of environmental parameters on marathon running performance. PLoS ONE 7(5):e37407, 2012 primary
  • Ely MR et al. Impact of weather on marathon-running performance. Med Sci Sports Exerc 39(3):487-493, 2007 primary
