Relative Effort

A heart-rate training load for one run, scored against your own last 6 weeks. A hard week makes today score lower for the same effort.

By Parth DixitPublished 1 September 2026

What it is

A training load built from heart rate, then compared with your own recent history. The underlying number is a weighted sum of time in each zone, in the style Banister described in 1980.

The comparison is what makes it relative. The same 10 km (6.2 mi) run scores differently depending on what your last six weeks looked like.

What it is not

It is not how hard the run felt to you. Runners put this plainly: _my body does not care if it is Monday_. The metric compares you to a rolling baseline, and your legs do not.

It is also not comparable between people. Two runners on the same run get different scores because their zones and their baselines differ.

What moves it

  • Time in the higher zones. The dominant term.
  • Your zone boundaries. A wrong maximum shifts every run's score.
  • Your recent load. A hard block lowers today's number.
  • Heat. More beats at the same pace means a higher score.

When to ignore it

Ignore it on a single run. The baseline makes one number close to meaningless in isolation.

Ignore it when your heart-rate trace has gaps or spikes. Zone time computed from a broken signal is a broken score.
