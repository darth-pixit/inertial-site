Glossary

Negative split

Running the second half of a race faster than the first. A 2% split is modest and achievable; most amateur marathons are run the other way round.

By Parth DixitPublished 1 September 2026

What it is

Second half quicker than the first. In a marathon at 4:00:00, a 2% negative split means 2:01:12 out and 1:58:48 back.

It is rare in amateur fields. Deaner and colleagues studied 14 marathons and found most runners slowed, men more than women.

What it is not

It is not slow running. The first half is barely different from even pace: 72 seconds across two hours, which is 6 s/km (10 s/mi). That margin is the whole difference in most sub-4 marathon attempts.

It is also not a guarantee. Starting slower does not create fitness. It stops you spending fitness you do not have.

What moves it

  • The first 5 km. Almost the whole decision.
  • Crowd and adrenaline. Both push you faster than planned.
  • Heat. Warm days punish a fast start harder.
  • Course profile. A downhill first half hides the error.

When to ignore it

Ignore the target on a course with a big net climb in the second half. Judge on effort instead.

Ignore it in a 5K, where the race is short enough that a hard, even effort is the whole strategy.
