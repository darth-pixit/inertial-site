Glossary

VO2max estimate

Your watch guessing your aerobic ceiling from pace and heart rate. Wearable estimates typically land within about 5 ml/kg/min of a lab measurement.

By Parth DixitPublished 1 September 2026

What it is

An estimate of how much oxygen you can use per kilogram per minute at full effort. A lab measures it with a mask. A watch infers it from how fast you run at a given heart rate.

A higher number means you go faster at the same heart rate. That is the whole signal.

RunnerTypical estimate
new runner, 40s30-38 ml/kg/min
regular club runner45-55 ml/kg/min
3-hour marathoner55-62 ml/kg/min
elite70-85 ml/kg/min

What it is not

It is not a lab result. Passler and colleagues found wrist devices land within roughly 5 ml/kg/min of a proper test, which is enough to be wrong about your category.

It is also not a ranking of you as a runner. Two people with the same figure can race minutes apart, because running economy and endurance differ.

What moves it

  • Training, slowly. Real change takes months.
  • Heat. A hot run raises heart rate, so the estimate drops.
  • Hills and wind. Both make you look slower at the same effort.
  • A wrong maximum heart rate. Everything downstream inherits it.

When to ignore it

Ignore a fall after a hot week or an illness. The estimate is measuring conditions, not fitness.

Ignore it entirely in the first month with a new watch. It has not seen enough of you yet.

Sources

  • Cooper KH. A means of assessing maximal oxygen intake. JAMA 203(3):201-204, 1968 primary
  • Uth N et al. Estimation of VO2max from the ratio between HRmax and HRrest. Eur J Appl Physiol 91(1):111-115, 2004 primary
  • Passler S et al. Validity of wrist-worn activity trackers for estimating VO2max. Int J Environ Res Public Health 16(17):3037, 2019 primary
