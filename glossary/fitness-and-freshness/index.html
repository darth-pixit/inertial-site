Fitness and freshness

Fitness is a rolling average of your training load, usually over 42 days. Freshness is fitness minus a shorter 7-day average, so it goes negative in hard weeks.

By Parth DixitPublished 1 September 2026

What it is

Two lines drawn from the same data.

Fitness is a long rolling average of your daily training load. Most systems use 42 days. It rises slowly when you train more than usual and falls slowly when you train less.

Freshness is fitness minus a short average, usually 7 days. When this week was harder than the last six, freshness is negative. When you taper, it climbs.

What it is not

It is not a measure of your fitness. It is a measure of how much you have been training. Those track each other loosely and come apart often.

This is the source of the most common complaint about the metric: _my fitness score fell while I got faster_. Both things can be true. If you replace long easy runs with short sharp ones, your load falls and your 5K time improves. The long version of that answer is here.

It is also not comparable between people. The number depends on how your intensity is measured and on your own baseline.

What moves it

  • Volume, mostly. Long runs raise fitness more than hard short ones.
  • Consistency. A missed week costs more than a great single session gains.
  • Your zones. If load is heart-rate based, wrong zones bend both lines.
  • Nothing you did today. With a 42-day window, one run moves fitness by about 2%.

When to ignore it

Ignore freshness during a taper, unless you are using it to confirm the taper is working. A positive number there is the plan, not a warning.

Ignore fitness after any break longer than a fortnight. It is decaying at the rate of a formula, not at the rate of your body.

Ignore both if your heart-rate data is unreliable. Two lines drawn from bad inputs are still bad inputs.
