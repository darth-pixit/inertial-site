Glossary

Taper

Cutting training volume before a race while keeping some intensity. Meta-analysis puts the typical gain near 3% over a 2-week taper.

By Parth DixitPublished 1 September 2026

What it is

A planned reduction in training before a race. Volume drops sharply. Intensity mostly stays.

Bosquet and colleagues pooled 27 studies in 2007. The pattern that worked best cut volume by 41 to 60% over about two weeks, kept the intensity, and left frequency roughly alone.

volumecut 40-60%this is where recovery comes from
intensitykeep itdropping it loses sharpness
frequencykeep it, or cut slightlyrhythm matters
duration8-14 dayslonger stops helping

What it is not

It is not rest. A taper with no fast running leaves most people feeling flat on race day.

It is also not a fitness gain. You are not getting fitter. You are arriving able to use what you already built.

What moves it

  • How much volume you cut. The main lever.
  • How long you take. Two weeks suits most marathon plans.
  • What you did before it. A bigger block needs a bigger taper.

When to ignore it

Ignore how you feel in the middle of it. Heavy legs and a high resting heart rate in taper week one are common and mean little.

Ignore the urge to add a session because you feel fresh. That is the taper working.

Sources
