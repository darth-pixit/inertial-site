Racing smarter

What pace is a 1:30 half marathon?

1:30:00 for the half marathon is 4:16/km (6:52/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 45:00. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 4:12/km.

What pace is a 1:30 half marathon?

1:30:00 over 21.0975 km (13.11 mi) is 4:16/km (6:52/mi), held from the first step to the last. There is no rounding slack in that number: run the first kilometre at 4:26/km and you have already spent ten seconds you will not get back cheaply.

A 90-minute half is 4:16/km (6:52/mi) and it is the classic club-runner benchmark. It usually needs a 5K under 20 minutes and 50 to 70 km a week. The distinctive thing about this target is that the pace sits very close to threshold for most people attempting it, which means there is almost no margin for a bad first 5 km. Go out at 4:05/km and you will not be able to recover inside the distance the way you could in a marathon. The reliable execution is boring: first 5 km at 4:18, middle 10 km at 4:16, last 6 km on whatever is left. People who do that run 1:29 and people who chase it early run 1:33.

Where should I be at halfway?

Even pace puts you through halfway at 45:00. Most people who finish this time went through slightly slower than that, not faster — a 2% negative split means halfway at 45:54 and a second half of 44:06.

The decision rule for race day: if you reach halfway more than 0:54 ahead of 45:00, you are not ahead of schedule, you are borrowing. Ease back to 4:16/km (6:52/mi) and hold it.

What if my watch measures the course long?

Almost every watch does. A half marathon that reads 1.5% long on your wrist means you have to run 4:12/km by the watch to finish in 1:30:00 by the clock. That is the pace to put on the screen, and it is why pacing off a watch's average-pace field alone tends to leave people a minute or two short.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 1:30 half marathon a good time?
Good is a comparison, and it depends who with. Whether it is achievable for you is the answerable question: it comes down to a recent race, your weekly volume and your longest run. The race predictor puts numbers on all three.
Should I run even splits or negative splits?
Even, planned; slightly negative, executed. Going through halfway 1-2% slower than even pace costs almost nothing if you are strong at the end, and saves the race if you are not.
