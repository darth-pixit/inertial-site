Racing smarter

What pace is a 2:15 half marathon?

2:15:00 for the half marathon is 6:24/km (10:18/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 1:07:30. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 6:18/km.

The pace in min/km and min/mi

2:15:00 over 21.0975 km (13.11 mi) is 6:24/km (10:18/mi). Run every kilometre 10 seconds slower than that and you finish 3:31 late, because 10 seconds times 21.0975 km is 3:31.

2:15 asks for 6:24/km (10:18/mi) and it is a realistic goal off three runs a week. At this pace most people are running at a genuine conversational effort for the first hour, which changes what the race is about. It becomes a fuelling and mental-focus event rather than a speed one. Two practical points. Take something to eat around 60 minutes: at this duration the difference between eating and not is visible in the last 5 km. And expect the middle 5 km to be the quiet part of the course where pace drifts most, because the early excitement has gone and the finish is not close yet. That is the stretch to check your watch on.

Halfway

Even pace puts you through halfway at 1:07:30. A 2% negative split is halfway at 1:08:51 and a second half of 1:06:09. More than 1:21 ahead of 1:07:30 is borrowed time, not a cushion. The idea is a negative split, planned even and only slightly negative if you run it at all.

The watch trace

On a half marathon, 1.5% of 2:15:00 is 2:02. The watch pace that still finishes in 2:15:00 is 6:18/km (10:09/mi). The longer explanation sits on the half marathon pace charts. Why the distance grows is GPS accuracy.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 2:15 half marathon a good time?
The page answers the pace, 6:24/km (10:18/mi), not whether the time is yours. A recent half, or a 10K plus the volume behind it, is the evidence. The race predictor prints three models so you can see how far apart they sit.
Should I run even splits or negative splits?
Plan even at 6:24/km (10:18/mi). A 2% negative split on the half is a small gap at halfway, not a minute taken out of the first 5 km (3.11 mi).
