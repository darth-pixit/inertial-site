What pace is a 35-minute 5K?

35:00 for the 5K is 7:00/km (11:16/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 17:30. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 6:54/km.

The pace in min/km and min/mi

35:00 over 5 km (3.11 mi) is 7:00/km (11:16/mi). Run every kilometre 10 seconds slower than that and you finish 0:50 late, because 10 seconds times 5 km is 0:50.

35 minutes is 7:00/km (11:16/mi), and it is a real 5K time run by a very large number of people every Saturday morning. It is also close to the pace at which many people finish a beginner programme, which makes it a better place to start measuring progress than the 30-minute mark everyone quotes. Two things help most at this pace. Running the first kilometre slower than the rest, because at 35 minutes the start-line surge costs more than it does at faster times. And walking briefly when you need it rather than stopping altogether: a 30-second walk at 3 km usually produces a faster finish than pushing through and fading.

Halfway

Halfway is 2.5 km. Even pace puts the clock at 17:30. A negative split here is a few seconds, not a minute. More than 0:21 ahead of that clock means you started too fast for this chart. The idea is a negative split, planned even and only slightly negative if you run it at all.

The watch trace

On a 5K, 1.5% of 35:00 is 0:32. The watch pace that still finishes in 35:00 is 6:54/km (11:06/mi). The longer explanation sits on the 5K pace charts. Why the distance grows is GPS accuracy.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 35-minute 5K a good time?
On a 5K, good is who you compare yourself with. This page gives the pace, 7:00/km (11:16/mi). Whether you can hold it comes from a recent race at a similar distance, not from a marathon formula.
Should I run even splits?
Yes. On a 5K a negative split is a few seconds. Starting faster than 7:00/km (11:16/mi) is how the time on this page slips away.
