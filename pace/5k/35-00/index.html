What pace is a 35-minute 5K?

35:00 for the 5K is 7:00/km (11:16/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 17:30. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 6:54/km.

What pace is a 35-minute 5K?

35:00 over 5 km (3.11 mi) is 7:00/km (11:16/mi), held from the first step to the last. There is no rounding slack in that number: run the first kilometre at 7:10/km and you have already spent ten seconds you will not get back cheaply.

35 minutes is 7:00/km (11:16/mi), and it is a real 5K time run by a very large number of people every Saturday morning. It is also close to the pace at which many people finish a beginner programme, which makes it a better place to start measuring progress than the 30-minute mark everyone quotes. Two things help most at this pace. Running the first kilometre slower than the rest, because at 35 minutes the start-line surge costs more than it does at faster times. And walking briefly when you need it rather than stopping altogether: a 30-second walk at 3 km usually produces a faster finish than pushing through and fading.

Where should I be at halfway?

Even pace puts you through halfway at 17:30. Most people who finish this time went through slightly slower than that, not faster — a 2% negative split means halfway at 17:51 and a second half of 17:09.

The decision rule for race day: if you reach halfway more than 0:21 ahead of 17:30, you are not ahead of schedule, you are borrowing. Ease back to 7:00/km (11:16/mi) and hold it.

What if my watch measures the course long?

Almost every watch does. A 5K that reads 1.5% long on your wrist means you have to run 6:54/km by the watch to finish in 35:00 by the clock. That is the pace to put on the screen, and it is why pacing off a watch's average-pace field alone tends to leave people a minute or two short.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 35-minute 5K a good time?
Good is a comparison, and it depends who with. Whether it is achievable for you is the answerable question: it comes down to a recent race, your weekly volume and your longest run. The race predictor puts numbers on all three.
Should I run even splits or negative splits?
Even, planned; slightly negative, executed. Going through halfway 1-2% slower than even pace costs almost nothing if you are strong at the end, and saves the race if you are not.
