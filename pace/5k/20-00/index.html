Racing smarter

What pace is a 20-minute 5K?

20:00 for the 5K is 4:00/km (6:26/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 10:00. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 3:56/km.

The pace in min/km and min/mi

20:00 over 5 km (3.11 mi) is 4:00/km (6:26/mi). Run every kilometre 10 seconds slower than that and you finish 0:50 late, because 10 seconds times 5 km is 0:50.

Sub-20 is 4:00/km (6:26/mi) and it is the benchmark that defines a whole tier of parkrun ambition. It needs real speed work, not just volume: most people who get there are running intervals at 3:45 pace weekly on top of 40 to 55 km. The race itself is almost pure discipline. Twenty minutes at this intensity gives you no recovery, so the first kilometre decides it. Start at 3:52 because the crowd is fast and you feel good, and the fourth kilometre will be 4:15. Start at 4:02, hold 4:00 through 3 km, and you will have something left for the last 800 metres, which is where sub-20 is actually won.

Halfway

Halfway is 2.5 km. Even pace puts the clock at 10:00. A negative split here is a few seconds, not a minute. More than 0:12 ahead of that clock means you started too fast for this chart. The idea is a negative split, planned even and only slightly negative if you run it at all.

The watch trace

On a 5K, 1.5% of 20:00 is 0:18. The watch pace that still finishes in 20:00 is 3:56/km (6:21/mi). The longer explanation sits on the 5K pace charts. Why the distance grows is GPS accuracy.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 20-minute 5K a good time?
On a 5K, good is who you compare yourself with. This page gives the pace, 4:00/km (6:26/mi). Whether you can hold it comes from a recent race at a similar distance, not from a marathon formula.
Should I run even splits?
Yes. On a 5K a negative split is a few seconds. Starting faster than 4:00/km (6:26/mi) is how the time on this page slips away.
