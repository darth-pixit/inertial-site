Racing smarter

What pace is a 23-minute 5K?

22:30 for the 5K is 4:30/km (7:15/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 11:15. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 4:26/km.

The pace in min/km and min/mi

22:30 over 5 km (3.11 mi) is 4:30/km (7:15/mi). Run every kilometre 10 seconds slower than that and you finish 0:50 late, because 10 seconds times 5 km is 0:50.

22:30 asks for 4:30/km (7:14/mi). It is the stage where most runners first meet the idea that a 5K is a hard race rather than a short one. The training that gets people here is usually three to four runs a week with one faster session, and the total volume matters less than the consistency. What is distinctive about this target is how much a warm-up is worth. At 22 minutes, the first kilometre is a large fraction of the race, and starting cold typically costs 10 to 15 seconds you cannot get back. Fifteen minutes of easy jogging with a few short accelerations before the start is the cheapest improvement available at this distance.

Halfway

Halfway is 2.5 km. Even pace puts the clock at 11:15. A negative split here is a few seconds, not a minute. More than 0:14 ahead of that clock means you started too fast for this chart. The idea is a negative split, planned even and only slightly negative if you run it at all.

The watch trace

On a 5K, 1.5% of 22:30 is 0:20. The watch pace that still finishes in 22:30 is 4:26/km (7:08/mi). The longer explanation sits on the 5K pace charts. Why the distance grows is GPS accuracy.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 23-minute 5K a good time?
On a 5K, good is who you compare yourself with. This page gives the pace, 4:30/km (7:15/mi). Whether you can hold it comes from a recent race at a similar distance, not from a marathon formula.
Should I run even splits?
Yes. On a 5K a negative split is a few seconds. Starting faster than 4:30/km (7:15/mi) is how the time on this page slips away.
