Racing smarter

What pace is a 4:15 marathon?

4:15:00 for the marathon is 6:03/km (9:44/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 2:07:30. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 5:57/km.

The pace in min/km and min/mi

4:15:00 over 42.195 km (26.22 mi) is 6:03/km (9:44/mi). Run every kilometre 10 seconds slower than that and you finish 7:02 late, because 10 seconds times 42.195 km is 7:02.

4:15 is often a sub-four attempt that has been sensibly revised, and it is usually the smarter race. At 6:02/km (9:43/mi) the effort is genuinely conversational for the first two hours for most people who have done the long runs. That changes the tactical question. When the pace is comfortable early, the risk is not blowing up, it is drifting. Runners at this target commonly run the first half at 4:05 pace without noticing, because it feels the same, and then pay for it at 30 km. Check your watch at 5 km and again at 10 km, and if you are more than 40 seconds ahead of schedule, slow down. Being on pace at halfway is worth more than any cushion.

Halfway

Even pace puts you through halfway at 2:07:30. A 2% negative split is halfway at 2:10:03 and a second half of 2:04:57. More than 2:33 ahead of 2:07:30 is borrowed time, not a cushion. The idea is a negative split, planned even and only slightly negative if you run it at all.

The watch trace

On a marathon the trace is often 1 to 1.5% long, which is minutes, not seconds. The watch pace for this time is 5:57/km (9:35/mi). The worked example for a 4:00:00 marathon, 5:36/km rather than 5:41/km, is on the marathon pace charts. Why the distance grows is GPS accuracy.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 4:15 marathon a good time?
The pace is 6:03/km (9:44/mi), held for 42.195 km (26.22 mi). Whether that is realistic depends on a recent half, weekly volume and the longest run. The race predictor shows three published models side by side, and they do not agree.
Should I run even splits or negative splits?
Even, planned, at 6:03/km (9:44/mi). Slightly slower than even at halfway if you still feel good. Going out faster than even is how marathon attempts come apart after 30 km (18.6 mi).
