Racing smarter

What pace is a 3:45 marathon?

3:45:00 for the marathon is 5:20/km (8:35/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 1:52:30. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 5:15/km.

What pace is a 3:45 marathon?

3:45:00 over 42.195 km (26.22 mi) is 5:20/km (8:35/mi), held from the first step to the last. There is no rounding slack in that number: run the first kilometre at 5:30/km and you have already spent ten seconds you will not get back cheaply.

3:45 sits in an awkward gap. It is too fast to treat as a survival run and too slow to be a speed problem, so it is almost entirely an endurance and pacing question. Most people who run it train four to five days a week at 45 to 60 km. The pace is 5:20/km (8:34/mi), which is close enough to many runners' comfortable long-run pace that it feels wrong in a good way for the first hour. That is the trap. A 3:45 attempt is usually lost between 28 and 34 km, and it is lost by two minutes of enthusiasm in the first hour. Run the first 10 km 10 seconds a kilometre slower than goal and you will pass people all afternoon.

Where should I be at halfway?

Even pace puts you through halfway at 1:52:30. Most people who finish this time went through slightly slower than that, not faster — a 2% negative split means halfway at 1:54:45 and a second half of 1:50:15.

The decision rule for race day: if you reach halfway more than 2:15 ahead of 1:52:30, you are not ahead of schedule, you are borrowing. Ease back to 5:20/km (8:35/mi) and hold it.

What if my watch measures the course long?

Almost every watch does. A marathon that reads 1.5% long on your wrist means you have to run 5:15/km by the watch to finish in 3:45:00 by the clock. That is the pace to put on the screen, and it is why pacing off a watch's average-pace field alone tends to leave people a minute or two short.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 3:45 marathon a good time?
Good is a comparison, and it depends who with. Whether it is achievable for you is the answerable question: it comes down to a recent race, your weekly volume and your longest run. The race predictor puts numbers on all three.
Should I run even splits or negative splits?
Even, planned; slightly negative, executed. Going through halfway 1-2% slower than even pace costs almost nothing if you are strong at the end, and saves the race if you are not.
