Racing smarter

What pace is a 3:30 marathon?

3:30:00 for the marathon is 4:59/km (8:01/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 1:45:00. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 4:54/km.

The pace in min/km and min/mi

3:30:00 over 42.195 km (26.22 mi) is 4:59/km (8:01/mi). Run every kilometre 10 seconds slower than that and you finish 7:02 late, because 10 seconds times 42.195 km is 7:02.

A 3:30 marathon is the most common serious target after a first finish. It is also where the maths gets unforgiving: you need roughly a 1:38 half, and most people attempting it have run a 1:45. The training behind it is usually 50 to 70 km a week with one long run over 28 km. The failure mode is specific. Runners hold the pace comfortably to 25 km, then lose 10 to 15 seconds a kilometre over the last 10 km and finish around 3:36. That is not the wall. That is starting at a pace your endurance supported for 25 km and not 42. If your half time is not under 1:40, plan the day around 3:35 and be pleased.

Halfway

Even pace puts you through halfway at 1:45:00. A 2% negative split is halfway at 1:47:06 and a second half of 1:42:54. More than 2:06 ahead of 1:45:00 is borrowed time, not a cushion. The idea is a negative split, planned even and only slightly negative if you run it at all.

The watch trace

On a marathon the trace is often 1 to 1.5% long, which is minutes, not seconds. The watch pace for this time is 4:54/km (7:53/mi). The worked example for a 4:00:00 marathon, 5:36/km rather than 5:41/km, is on the marathon pace charts. Why the distance grows is GPS accuracy.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 3:30 marathon a good time?
The pace is 4:59/km (8:01/mi), held for 42.195 km (26.22 mi). Whether that is realistic depends on a recent half, weekly volume and the longest run. The race predictor shows three published models side by side, and they do not agree.
Should I run even splits or negative splits?
Even, planned, at 4:59/km (8:01/mi). Slightly slower than even at halfway if you still feel good. Going out faster than even is how marathon attempts come apart after 30 km (18.6 mi).
