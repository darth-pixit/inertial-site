What pace is a 50-minute 10K?

50:00 for the 10K is 5:00/km (8:03/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 25:00. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 4:56/km.

The pace in min/km and min/mi

50:00 over 10 km (6.21 mi) is 5:00/km (8:03/mi). Run every kilometre 10 seconds slower than that and you finish 1:40 late, because 10 seconds times 10 km is 1:40.

50 minutes needs 5:00/km (8:03/mi), which makes it the easiest 10K goal to pace: every kilometre is five minutes, and you can do the arithmetic while running. It is a very common first target after a parkrun habit settles, and it typically follows a 5K around 24 minutes. The specific risk here is the parkrun instinct. People who race 5K hard often start a 10K at 4:45 because that is the pace their body associates with a race effort, then find the second half is a different event. Deliberately run the first kilometre at 5:05. You will still finish under 50 if the rest is even, and you will enjoy the last 2 km instead of surviving them.

Halfway

Halfway is 5 km. Even pace puts the clock at 25:00. A negative split here is a few seconds, not a minute. More than 0:30 ahead of that clock means you started too fast for this chart. The idea is a negative split, planned even and only slightly negative if you run it at all.

The watch trace

On a 10K, 1.5% of 50:00 is 0:45. The watch pace that still finishes in 50:00 is 4:56/km (7:56/mi). The longer explanation sits on the 10K pace charts. Why the distance grows is GPS accuracy.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 50-minute 10K a good time?
On a 10K, good is who you compare yourself with. This page gives the pace, 5:00/km (8:03/mi). Whether you can hold it comes from a recent race at a similar distance, not from a marathon formula.
Should I run even splits?
Yes. On a 10K a negative split is a few seconds. Starting faster than 5:00/km (8:03/mi) is how the time on this page slips away.
