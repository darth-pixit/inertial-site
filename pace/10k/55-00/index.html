Racing smarter

What pace is a 55-minute 10K?

55:00 for the 10K is 5:30/km (8:51/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 27:30. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 5:25/km.

What pace is a 55-minute 10K?

55:00 over 10 km (6.21 mi) is 5:30/km (8:51/mi), held from the first step to the last. There is no rounding slack in that number: run the first kilometre at 5:40/km and you have already spent ten seconds you will not get back cheaply.

55 minutes is 5:30/km (8:51/mi). It is a solid, unremarkable, completely normal 10K time, and it is worth saying that because most pages about 10K times imply otherwise. At this pace, the effort for a trained runner is somewhere between comfortable and steady, which means the race is decided by how well you hold concentration between 4 and 8 km rather than by how hard you can push. The other thing that matters at this target is warm-up. Ten minutes of easy jogging before the start is worth more here than at any faster time, because a cold first kilometre at 5:50 is nearly impossible to claw back in a race this short.

Where should I be at halfway?

Even pace puts you through halfway at 27:30. Most people who finish this time went through slightly slower than that, not faster — a 2% negative split means halfway at 28:03 and a second half of 26:57.

The decision rule for race day: if you reach halfway more than 0:33 ahead of 27:30, you are not ahead of schedule, you are borrowing. Ease back to 5:30/km (8:51/mi) and hold it.

What if my watch measures the course long?

Almost every watch does. A 10K that reads 1.5% long on your wrist means you have to run 5:25/km by the watch to finish in 55:00 by the clock. That is the pace to put on the screen, and it is why pacing off a watch's average-pace field alone tends to leave people a minute or two short.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 55-minute 10K a good time?
Good is a comparison, and it depends who with. Whether it is achievable for you is the answerable question: it comes down to a recent race, your weekly volume and your longest run. The race predictor puts numbers on all three.
Should I run even splits or negative splits?
Even, planned; slightly negative, executed. Going through halfway 1-2% slower than even pace costs almost nothing if you are strong at the end, and saves the race if you are not.
