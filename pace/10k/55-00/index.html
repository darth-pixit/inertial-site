What pace is a 55-minute 10K?

55:00 for the 10K is 5:30/km (8:51/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 27:30. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 5:25/km.

The pace in min/km and min/mi

55:00 over 10 km (6.21 mi) is 5:30/km (8:51/mi). Run every kilometre 10 seconds slower than that and you finish 1:40 late, because 10 seconds times 10 km is 1:40.

55 minutes is 5:30/km (8:51/mi). It is a solid, unremarkable, completely normal 10K time, and it is worth saying that because most pages about 10K times imply otherwise. At this pace, the effort for a trained runner is somewhere between comfortable and steady, which means the race is decided by how well you hold concentration between 4 and 8 km rather than by how hard you can push. The other thing that matters at this target is warm-up. Ten minutes of easy jogging before the start is worth more here than at any faster time, because a cold first kilometre at 5:50 is nearly impossible to claw back in a race this short.

Halfway

Halfway is 5 km. Even pace puts the clock at 27:30. A negative split here is a few seconds, not a minute. More than 0:33 ahead of that clock means you started too fast for this chart. The idea is a negative split, planned even and only slightly negative if you run it at all.

The watch trace

On a 10K, 1.5% of 55:00 is 0:50. The watch pace that still finishes in 55:00 is 5:25/km (8:43/mi). The longer explanation sits on the 10K pace charts. Why the distance grows is GPS accuracy.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 55-minute 10K a good time?
On a 10K, good is who you compare yourself with. This page gives the pace, 5:30/km (8:51/mi). Whether you can hold it comes from a recent race at a similar distance, not from a marathon formula.
Should I run even splits?
Yes. On a 10K a negative split is a few seconds. Starting faster than 5:30/km (8:51/mi) is how the time on this page slips away.
