Racing smarter

What pace is a 60-minute 10K?

1:00:00 for the 10K is 6:00/km (9:39/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 30:00. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 5:55/km.

What pace is a 60-minute 10K?

1:00:00 over 10 km (6.21 mi) is 6:00/km (9:39/mi), held from the first step to the last. There is no rounding slack in that number: run the first kilometre at 6:10/km and you have already spent ten seconds you will not get back cheaply.

The one-hour 10K is 6:00/km (9:39/mi) and it is the most popular first-10K goal there is. The pleasant thing about it is that the arithmetic checks itself: every kilometre marker should tick over on the minute. If you are at 24:30 at 4 km you are ahead, and if you are at 25:30 you have a decision to make with plenty of race left. Most people chasing this have run a 5K around 28 minutes. The failure mode is treating it as a long 5K. It is not: the last 3 km of a one-hour 10K feel like a different event, and the runners who get there fresh are the ones who ran 6:05 for the first two kilometres and did not panic.

Where should I be at halfway?

Even pace puts you through halfway at 30:00. Most people who finish this time went through slightly slower than that, not faster — a 2% negative split means halfway at 30:36 and a second half of 29:24.

The decision rule for race day: if you reach halfway more than 0:36 ahead of 30:00, you are not ahead of schedule, you are borrowing. Ease back to 6:00/km (9:39/mi) and hold it.

What if my watch measures the course long?

Almost every watch does. A 10K that reads 1.5% long on your wrist means you have to run 5:55/km by the watch to finish in 1:00:00 by the clock. That is the pace to put on the screen, and it is why pacing off a watch's average-pace field alone tends to leave people a minute or two short.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 60-minute 10K a good time?
Good is a comparison, and it depends who with. Whether it is achievable for you is the answerable question: it comes down to a recent race, your weekly volume and your longest run. The race predictor puts numbers on all three.
Should I run even splits or negative splits?
Even, planned; slightly negative, executed. Going through halfway 1-2% slower than even pace costs almost nothing if you are strong at the end, and saves the race if you are not.
