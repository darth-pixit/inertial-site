Racing smarter

What pace is a 60-minute 10K?

1:00:00 for the 10K is 6:00/km (9:39/mi), held the whole way. Halfway on even pace is 30:00. Most watches measure the course long, so the pace you actually need on the screen is nearer 5:55/km.

The pace in min/km and min/mi

1:00:00 over 10 km (6.21 mi) is 6:00/km (9:39/mi). Run every kilometre 10 seconds slower than that and you finish 1:40 late, because 10 seconds times 10 km is 1:40.

The one-hour 10K is 6:00/km (9:39/mi) and it is the most popular first-10K goal there is. The pleasant thing about it is that the arithmetic checks itself: every kilometre marker should tick over on the minute. If you are at 24:30 at 4 km you are ahead, and if you are at 25:30 you have a decision to make with plenty of race left. Most people chasing this have run a 5K around 28 minutes. The failure mode is treating it as a long 5K. It is not: the last 3 km of a one-hour 10K feel like a different event, and the runners who get there fresh are the ones who ran 6:05 for the first two kilometres and did not panic.

Halfway

Halfway is 5 km. Even pace puts the clock at 30:00. A negative split here is a few seconds, not a minute. More than 0:36 ahead of that clock means you started too fast for this chart. The idea is a negative split, planned even and only slightly negative if you run it at all.

The watch trace

On a 10K, 1.5% of 1:00:00 is 0:54. The watch pace that still finishes in 1:00:00 is 5:55/km (9:31/mi). The longer explanation sits on the 10K pace charts. Why the distance grows is GPS accuracy.

Questions runners actually ask

Is a 60-minute 10K a good time?
On a 10K, good is who you compare yourself with. This page gives the pace, 6:00/km (9:39/mi). Whether you can hold it comes from a recent race at a similar distance, not from a marathon formula.
Should I run even splits?
Yes. On a 10K a negative split is a few seconds. Starting faster than 6:00/km (9:39/mi) is how the time on this page slips away.
